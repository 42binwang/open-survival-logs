# ART.md: Survival Log look standard (WP-P0-04)

This is the production standard for every art lane: camera, scale, texel density, lighting, bake contract, tone
mapping, grade, post effects and material bands.

Every number carries one of four labels:

- **measured**: a reference surface or frame; the script and output file are named.
- **derived**: computed from measured values; the formula is given.
- **decided**: a choice; the reason is given.
- **provisional**: rests on weak or HUD-less evidence and must be re-checked; also listed in §11.

If a render disagrees with this file, fix the render, or change this file with new measurements.

- The references are in `assets/targets/source/`:
  - 8 Steam screenshots;
  - 30 evenly spaced trailer frames and 49 per-shot trailer frames;
  - `manifest.json` with source URL, time code and SHA-256 for every file.

  They are **look reference only**: never traced, never shipped, never part of the build. © Midnight Workshop /
  Lilith Games.
- Raw outputs are in `tools/targets/out/*.json`, regenerated by the scripts in §10.
- Boards are in `docs/art/`. Mood boards in `docs/art/mood/` are AI concept images, never references.

All frame values are **display-referred**: what the source puts on screen after its own lighting, tone mapping and
grade.

- Trailer frames hold the 1920×1080 game frame scaled by 0.90625 at (90, 9) inside a paper border. Scripts map
  back with `game = (file − (90, 9)) / 0.90625`.
- **Native** frames show the game HUD. **Punch-ins** are HUD-less trailer shots: the editor cropped, rotated or
  panned the picture.
- Punch-ins never define a standard: not camera, not colour, not light direction, not vignette.

### 1.1 Measured (`tools/targets/camera.py` → `out/camera.json`, boards `docs/art/camera/vp_*.jpg`)

three.js, with x = east = sim x, z = south = sim y, y up, 1 unit = 1 m:

```js
const camera = new THREE.PerspectiveCamera(60, innerWidth / innerHeight, 1.0, 80);
const D = 10.47, pitch = THREE.MathUtils.degToRad(40), yaw = THREE.MathUtils.degToRad(30);
const offset = new THREE.Vector3(
  D * Math.sin(yaw) * Math.cos(pitch),   // +4.010 (east)
  D * Math.sin(pitch),                   // +6.730 (up)
  D * Math.cos(yaw) * Math.cos(pitch),   // +6.946 (south)
);
camera.position.copy(target).add(offset);
camera.lookAt(target);                  // target on the current floor plane
// zoom: camera.fov = lerp(60, 50, t); camera.updateProjectionMatrix();  // never move the camera to zoom
```

Controls (from `https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/4164790` and `docs/audit/playthrough.md`):

- Drag or WASD pans the target on the floor plane and stops following.
- Space recentres on the survivor.
- Holding `.` or `,` orbits the yaw around the survivor, and it springs back to 30° on release. The audit logged
  −0.32 with no unit. Treat it as radians, i.e. an 18° swivel limit (decided).
- Pitch never changes.

### 1.3 Cutaway rules

- **Camera-facing exterior walls** (south and east at home yaw) are cut to **0.9 m** and capped. Measured 0.89 m
  (t035).
- **Far exterior walls** (north and west) stay full height.
- **Interior walls** (decided rule): each segment has one face whose normal points towards the camera's horizontal
  position. The segment is cut to 0.9 m when the space behind that face (on the side away from the camera) is a
  room of the viewed floor; it stays full height when the space behind is outside or another floor.
  - This cuts every partition in t035's living area.
  - t035 also shows one full-height partition beside the kitchen, which the rule would cut. Home data therefore
    carries a per-wall `keepFullHeight` flag, set by the home author (P0-13) where the source shows a full wall.
- **Recompute on orbit.** "Camera-facing" is re-evaluated from the current yaw every frame.
- **Openings.** Door and window frames in cut walls stay full height: the security door stands whole in a cut wall
  in t035 and t041.
- **Hidden geometry.** Floors above the viewed floor, roofs, and neighbouring roofs are hidden (neighbouring roofs
  when the camera moves up; patch notes). Hidden geometry is not drawn but still **occludes light**; see §4.4.
- **Occlusion highlight.** Furniture occluded by full-height walls gets a darker see-through outline (patch notes).
  Alt highlights interactables.
- **Replaces iso.js.** `src/render/iso.js` (2:1 orthographic isometric, yaw 45°) is replaced by this camera. Only
  its "south and east walls low" logic carries over.

- **Tolerance.** ±10 % per UV island mean, checked by P0-11.
- **Texture sizes** (P0-05 KTX2 budget): a 512 texture covers 2 × 2 m at 256 px/m; a 1K hero texture covers
  2 × 2 m at 512 px/m.
- **Filtering.** Floors are seen at 10–70° incidence at 60° and 15–65° at 50°, so use 8× anisotropic filtering
  and mipmaps.
- **Tiling.** Tiled materials repeat at 1 m or 2 m, aligned to the grid.

## 4. Lighting

### 4.1 Units and calibration

- three.js r186, `toneMappingExposure = 1.0` for every rig, no eye adaptation.
- **E** is irradiance on the lit surface:
  - DirectionalLight intensity is E on a surface facing the light.
  - HemisphereLight, AmbientLight and lightmap texels are E.
  - Point and spot light intensity **I** is in candela-like units, with E = I / d² (`decay = 2`).
- A Lambertian surface of albedo ρ shows radiance ρ·E/π.
- Multiplying every intensity by one factor and dividing `toneMappingExposure` by the same factor gives the same
  image. The factor is a scale, not a conversion to lux.

The calibration (`tools/targets/lut.py` → `out/rigs.json`) inverts the display value of a reference through the
bit-exact AgX port (§5.1) and divides by an albedo. There are two kinds of anchor, and each rig says which it uses:

- **Surface anchors**: a known surface with an assumed albedo.
  - Plaster 0.60: t069 out of the lamp pool, and ss_04 at 17:20.
  - White tile 0.75: t041, and t035 (relative albedos only).
  - Roof concrete 0.35: t059, sunlit and in the dish shadow.
  - Plaster at night: t033.
- **Frame-statistic anchors**: a frame percentile read as a surface of albedo 0.30. Used for dawn (ss_06 median),
  unlit night corners (t033 p5) and storm night (ss_05 median). They are weaker than surface anchors (§11).
- **Unusable**: t013 (blackout). Its masked frame has a flat floor, p1 = 33.7 and p5 = 33.9: a screen-space
  darkness overlay, not scene light. t036 is a punch-in and is not used.
- **Relabelled**: t035 shows the planning-phase countdown (hourglass 03:28), not the clock. Its time of day is
  unknown, so it anchors nothing but relative albedos inside its own frame.

Directions are compass azimuths on the grid: 0° = north (−z), 90° = east (+x). The home view points to 330°. The
direction towards the light is `(sin az · cos el, sin el, −cos az · cos el)`.

Shadows (decided):

- One key-light shadow map, 2048², fitted to the viewed floor plus 4 m (about 1.5 cm per texel on a 30 m square),

- Penumbra in world units: day 0.05 m, dusk 0.10 m, night moon 0.10 m.

Transitions (decided):

- Every rig value interpolates linearly over 45 game-minutes centred on each boundary.
- The sun's azimuth **decreases monotonically**: dawn 75° → day 15° → dusk 285°, passing through 0° and 330°, so

- The moon is a separate light that fades in at dusk and out at dawn at its fixed 15° / 45°.

### 4.4 Bake and runtime contract (decided)

> **Superseded for the shells (2026-09-24).** The three.js renderer builds each home's shell procedurally
> (`src/render3d/shell.js`) and lights it in real time (`src/render3d/lighting.js`): no shell models and no
> lightmap sets are baked, and the art-metrics content check no longer asks for them. The rules below still apply
> to any baked asset a later lane ships.

The source shows no sun patches inside rooms at 11:50 (t069) or 17:20 (ss_04). Floors, ceilings and roofs that the
cutaway hides therefore still block the sun. Cutting the roof off without that occlusion would light interiors with
the full exterior level: the day exterior E of 2.22 is 5.3× the 0.419 interior target.

- **Hidden geometry still occludes.**
  - The bake always includes the full building: all floors, ceilings, roofs, and cut walls at full height.
  - At runtime, hidden floors and roofs are drawn into the key light's shadow map only: `castShadow = true`, with
    `colorWrite = false` and `depthWrite = false`, or a shadow-only layer. Cut walls cast their full-height
    shadow.
- **The key light's direct term is never baked.** `DAY`, `DUSK` and `NIGHT` hold the sky, the bounce, and the key
  light's **indirect** light only. In Cycles, the key (sun or moon) contributes indirect light only: disable its
  direct contribution to the lightmap bake, for example by baking with the key's direct pass excluded. The key's
  direct light on every surface comes only from the runtime shadowed DirectionalLight.
  - A bake that also held direct sun would double-count it. The sun is 86.9 % of horizontal E by day, so sunlit
    static surfaces would render at about 1.9× their target.
  - The reference-room normalisation cannot catch that error, because the reference room receives no direct sun.
- **Five lightmap sets per home.** Irradiance E per texel, in the units of §4.1.

  | set | contains | normalised so the reference room reads |
  |---|---|---|
  | `DAY` | sky + bounce + day-sun indirect; no direct sun | 0.419 |
  | `DUSK` = `DUSK_SKY` + `DUSK_SUN` | two passes: dusk sky + its bounce; dusk-sun indirect + its bounce. No direct sun; lamps off | **0.197** in total, **13 % from `DUSK_SUN`** (below) |
  | `NIGHT` | sky + bounce + moon indirect; no direct moon; lamps off | 0.101 |
  | `OVERCAST` | uniform overcast sky dome + its bounce, no sun (decided: overcast rigs must not reuse DAY's sun-bounce pattern) | 1.0 |
  | `LAMPS` | every fixed interior lamp (shaded, §4.5), direct + bounce, stored alone | calibrated in §4.5 |

  DUSK normalisation: the ss_04 anchor (0.237) already has the bed lamp on. DUSK is therefore 0.237 minus the LAMPS
  value at the ss_04 reference texel. The upper bound is 0.040 (the LAMPS share of t033's wall at 1–2 m), which
  gives 0.197. P0-08 replaces the 0.040 with the baked LAMPS value at that texel (`out/rigs.json`
  `rigs.dusk.interior_DUSK_set_E`, next to `interior_with_lamps_E` 0.237, as night splits 0.101 / 0.141).

  **Dusk interior colour (measured; changes what P0-13 bakes).** `lut.py dusk_interior_light` → `out/rigs.json`
  `rigs.dusk.interior_light`.
  - Method: three materials appear in the same home at 11:50 (t069) and 17:20 (ss_04): back-wall plaster, concrete
    floor, saw-table top. Their per-channel scene ratio (AgX inverted in RGB), times the day interior light as
    rigged (86.9 % 5500 K + 13.1 % 7500 K), gives the ss_04 interior light: 5730 K.
  - Removing the bed lamp's 0.040 at 2700 K gives the lamp-free DUSK light: `#f4fbff` (measured light, CCT **6900 K**; slightly off the blackbody curve, where a 6900 K blackbody would be `#f6f4ff`).
  - Fitted as a mix of the dusk sun (2600 K) and dusk sky (8000 K), that light is **13 % sun, 87 % sky**. Per pair:
    plaster 0 % (8620 K), floor 17 % (6490 K), table 25 % (5970 K).
  - A literal 13 % 2600 K + 87 % 8000 K mix is **6630 K**, 5.9 mired from 6900 K, which is below the visible
    threshold (`rigs.dusk.interior_light.literal_mix_cct_k`). A P0-13 bake built from the two passes at 13 / 87 is
    correct at 6630 K; do not flag it against 6900 K.
  - So the interior at dusk is sky-lit and near-neutral, not orange. Applying the rig's 87 % 2600 K sun literally
    is wrong indoors. The character lane's 40 / 60 is also outside the measured 0–25 % range.
  - **How the bake gets it:** bake `DUSK_SKY` and `DUSK_SUN` separately, then weight them so the reference room
    (the ss_04 texel) reads 0.197 in total with 13 % from `DUSK_SUN`. Store the weighted sum as `DUSK`, or keep
    both with fixed weights. Direct dusk sun still comes only from the runtime shadowed light, and only where it
    reaches (windows, the yard).

- **Per-rig mapping.** Lightmap E = weights on the sets. `LAMPS` has weight 0 whenever grid power is off.

  | rig | `DAY` | `DUSK` | `NIGHT` | `OVERCAST` | `LAMPS` |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | day | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
  | dawn | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.142 | 0 |
  | dusk | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 from 17:00 |
  | night with lamps | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 (0.101 + 0.040 = 0.141 on t033's wall) |
  | blackout | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
  | storm, day | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.441 | 0 |
  | storm, night | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
  | cold wave | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.441 | 0 |

  Transitions cross-fade the weights. Dawn keeps its weak sun as the runtime key light (§4.3), with no baked
  contribution.
- **Static lightmapped surfaces** get lightmap E plus the key light's direct term (sun or moon, shadowed) and
  nothing else. No HemisphereLight or AmbientLight on them, because the bake already holds the sky.
- **Movable objects and characters** get light probes baked from the same sets with the same weights (including
  `DUSK_SKY` / `DUSK_SUN`, so they get the same 6900 K dusk colour), plus the key light.
  - **Probes are directional.** Use SH L2 from the bake: vertical faces turned towards lit walls, windows and open
    rooms get more than faces turned away. Expect a front/back difference on the survivor, not the flat look of a
    hemisphere.
  - **Vertical-surface target, day interior** (derived, `out/rigs.json` `rigs.movables`): **E 0.419**. The
    day-interior anchor (plaster-t069-b) is itself a vertical wall, so a 0.60 card facing into the room must read
    85–95 codes.
  - Under that E the survivor's hoodie (albedo 0.281) displays about 59 codes as a flat card. The source shows a
    median of 51 (p10–p90 44–77) in t069. A render reading 38 front / 45 back with the hemisphere alone is
    under-lit by 0.55 EV (front) and 0.25 EV (back), from AgX inversion against 51. The probes are expected to close that gap.
  - **HemisphereLight fallback** (only where no probe exists), decided; generated in `out/rigs.json`
    `rigs.hemisphere_fallback`:
    - `groundColor` is 4000 K. `color` is the rig's interior light: day 5500 K, dusk the measured light (CCT 6900 K),
      night 8000 K.
    - **Both colours are normalised to unit luminance** (linear sRGB, set with `setRGB(..., LinearSRGBColorSpace)`).
      A vertical face of a hemisphere receives the mean of sky and ground, so it gets exactly `intensity`.
    - `intensity` = the rig's vertical target: day 0.419, dusk 0.197, night 0.101 (+ LAMPS where on). The script
      checks that vertical faces receive 0.419 / 0.197 / 0.101.
- **Dynamic lamps** exist only for lamps not in the bake (portable, player-placed). A baked lamp is never also
  dynamic; toggling a baked lamp changes its own channel of `LAMPS`. Bake `LAMPS` per room group so rooms can
  switch separately.

### 4.5 Lamps and flashlight (`measure.py lamps` → `out/lamps.json`, board `docs/art/lighting/lamps.jpg`; `lut.py` → `out/rigs.json` `rigs.night_lamps.lamps`)

Method:

1. Measure patches of the **lit surface** (the wall behind the lamp), each outside a 34 px mask that covers the
   emitter and its bloom.
2. Compare them with an ambient patch of the same plaster.
3. Invert display values through AgX to scene ratios.
4. Convert screen offsets to metres at the emitter's depth, undoing the wall foreshortening (vertical / cos 40°,
   horizontal / cos 30°).

| frame | patch | distance from bulb | scene contrast vs ambient |
|---|---|---|---|
| t069 (day, native) | wall beside the shade | 0.76 m | **1.82 : 1** |
| t069 | wall upper left | 1.83 m | 1.29 : 1 |
| t033 (night, native) | wall above the shade | 1.04 m | 1.03 : 1 |
| t033 | wall beside the racket | 2.16 m | 1.09 : 1 |

- **Intensity.** Assuming normal incidence on the wall, I = (C − 1) × E_ambient × d², with E_ambient = 0.419
  (day interior):
  - (1.82 − 1) × 0.419 × 0.76² = **0.198**;
  - (1.29 − 1) × 0.419 × 1.83² = **0.407**.
  - Table lamp **I = 0.284** (derived: geometric mean).
- **Falloff.** The two t069 patches fall off more slowly than inverse square, and the night wall in t033 is flat
  within 9 % over 1–2 m. The source's lamp pools are soft and bounce-dominated, so **no falloff radius is
  claimed**.
- **LAMPS calibration target for P0-08** (measured anchor; `out/rigs.json` `rigs.night_lamps.lamps.calibration_target`):
  - At weight 1 (NIGHT + LAMPS), t033's reference wall must read **E 0.141**: plaster 0.60 at **46.5 ± 5 codes**,
    i.e. E 0.122–0.162. NIGHT supplies 0.101 and LAMPS 0.040.
  - A bare point light at I 0.284 fails this. It puts 0.262 of direct E on the wall at 1.04 m (84 codes) and 0.061
    at 2.16 m, which predicts a 2.24 : 1 gradient where t033 is flat within 3–9 %.
  - Table lamps therefore get **shades that block sideways light** (decided, table above). The light leaves up and
    down, and the wall sees mostly bounce.
  - P0-08 tunes the bulb I, starting at 0.284, until the target holds. The tuned I is the standard.

**Emitter surfaces** (lamp shades, bulbs, fire cores, screens) are not lit surfaces. The t069 shade displays

(`out/grade.json` `emitters_unreachable_by_agx`). So emitters are drawn in an **emitter layer** (decided):

- Re-render the emitter meshes after the grade (§5.3) into the composer's buffer. The pass keeps **the main
  render's depth texture**: share the RenderPass target's `depthTexture`, `depthTest: true`, `depthWrite: false`.
- Emitters are unlit `MeshBasicMaterial`s writing **display-encoded** colour directly: `toneMapped: false`, and the
  colour set as the encoded values with no colour-space conversion. For `#daa850`, use
  `color.setRGB(0xda / 255, 0xa8 / 255, 0x50 / 255, THREE.LinearSRGBColorSpace)`.
- In the main scene the same meshes carry a scene-linear emissive that drives bloom (§6): `emissive` 2700 K
  `#ffad59`, **`emissiveIntensity` 3.0**. That is scene luminance 0.518 × 3.0 = 1.55, above the bloom threshold 1.0
  (decided; `out/rigs.json` `rigs.emitter`).

## 5. Tone mapping, exposure, grade

### 5.1 AgX and exposure

- `renderer.toneMapping = THREE.AgXToneMapping` (three.js r186 base; `tools/targets/lut.py` reproduces it
  bit-for-bit), `toneMappingExposure = 1.0`.
- Port facts: scene 0.18 displays at 0.50 (sRGB 128); display 0.98 is reached at scene 10.0; black stays black.
- Calibration check for P0-08: render a 0.18 grey card under each rig's interior target and exterior key. The card
  must land within ±5 codes of the §4.2 / §4.3 values. Adjust intensities, never exposure.

### 5.2 Grade LUT v2 (`assets/targets/lut/survival-log-grade-v2.cube`, `out/grade.json`)

- **Format.** 33³, domain 0..1, sRGB-encoded in and out. It validates with `node tools/targets/validate-lut.mjs`,
  both as spec parsing and through three.js r186 `LUTCubeLoader`.
- **What it does: one thing, the neutral bias.** Near-neutral surfaces under near-neutral light (overcast ss_06,
  snow t041) show a small warm-olive cast, which the LUT reproduces as Δ(a*, b*):

  | band | Δa* | Δb* |
  |---|---|---|
  | shadows | −0.43 | +1.79 |
  | mid-tones | −1.41 | +0.99 |
  | highlights | −1.48 | +0.71 |

  - It applies only where encoded chroma (max − min channel, 0–1 scale) is below 0.16. The bias fades to zero at
    black and white.
- **What v1 did, and why it is gone.** v1's light-source saturation boost is removed: it shifted bright diffuse
  colours (a sunlit tan by ΔE 23). Lamp colour now comes from the emitter layer (§4.5).
- Measured on the committed `.cube` with trilinear sampling, as LUTPass does (`out/grade.json` `stats`):

  | check | result |
  |---|---|
  | sunlit ColorChecker (24 patches at the day exterior E) | ΔE76 max **1.81**, mean 0.45 (limit 2.0: pass) |
  | sunlit tan `#d2aa78` | ΔE76 0.00 |
  | largest change anywhere | 3.07 codes |
  | 8 native screenshots | 54.5 % of pixels change by more than 2 codes; 0.0 % by more than 10 |
  | lookup error vs the exact grade | p99 0.10 / max 1.29 codes (uniform RGB); p99 0.65 / max 1.76 codes (synthetic scene colours) |

### 5.3 Pass order (decided)

```js
composer.addPass(new RenderPass(scene, camera));
composer.addPass(gtaoPass);                       // §6, scene-linear
composer.addPass(bloomPass);                      // §6, scene-linear
composer.addPass(new OutputPass());               // AgX + sRGB encode
composer.addPass(new LUTPass({ lut: (await new LUTCubeLoader().loadAsync('assets/targets/lut/survival-log-grade-v2.cube')).texture3D }));
composer.addPass(emitterPass);                    // §4.5 emitter layer, display-referred, depth-tested
composer.addPass(vignettePass);                   // §6, display-linear multiply (provisional strength)
composer.addPass(ditherPass);                     // §6, ±0.5 LSB blue noise, luminance only; last
```

The UI is DOM or a separate overlay: no tone mapping, LUT, vignette or dither.

## 6. Grain, vignette, bloom, ambient occlusion, fog

- **Grain: none** (measured).
  - `measure.py grain` looks at the flattest 16×16 scene patches of the native screenshots. JPEG luma quantisers
    are DC 3 / AC 2 (q ≥ 90), and the high-pass residual is **0.1–0.5 codes RMS**, below the flat UI panels'
    0.8 (median).
  - The upper bound on source grain is 0.3 codes. This is decided: at these quantisers, grain of more than about
    0.3 codes would survive into the residual.
  - Standard: no film grain; a **±0.5 LSB blue-noise dither**, luminance only, as the last pass.
- **Vignette: provisional, and a tunable.**
  - Re-fitted on camera pans in shots whose HUD stays on screen (a static right-hand HUD column): 11 pairs from 5
    shots; 23 pairs whose HUD moved were rejected (`out/vignette_pan.json`). The bootstrap resamples pairs and
    reruns the same robust fit.
  - Result: the estimate at the corner is 0.71, with a 68 % CI of 0.30–1.23, so "no vignette" cannot be excluded.
    The free two-term fit is non-physical in the middle (V(0.5) = 1.13).
  - Standard: P0-08 exposes `vignetteCorner` (display-linear multiplier at r = 1, with r = 1 at the frame corner,
    aspect-aware), default **0.71 (provisional)**. The shape is V(r) = 1 − (1 − corner) · r².
  - The art review sets the final value by eye against the native screenshots.
- **Bloom** (decided). `UnrealBloomPass` in scene-linear, threshold 1.0 (scene), **strength 0.25, radius 0.35**.
  - Threshold (derived): a 0.9-albedo diffuse white in full day sun reaches 0.9 × 2.22 / π = 0.64 scene, so only
    emitters (1.55, §4.5), the flashlight pool and fire bloom.
  - Strength reason: the source's halos are faint. The t069 wall 0.76 m from the shade reads only 1.82 : 1,
    including any bloom, so bloom must stay well below the lamp's own light. Strength 0.25 adds a quarter of the
    above-threshold energy: for a shade at 1.55, that is 0.14.
  - Radius reason: 0.35 on UnrealBloomPass's 0–1 mip blend weights the small mips, so the halo stays close to the
    emitter (t013's pool edge is sharp at 22° beam angle).
- **Ambient occlusion** (decided). **No GTAOPass.** The Cycles lightmaps already hold contact occlusion for static
  geometry, and three.js GTAOPass darkens the whole frame (direct light included).
  - AO is a **material term on probe-lit movables only**: each movable asset (furniture, props, characters) ships
    an `aoMap` baked in its own space, `aoMapIntensity` 1.0. three.js applies it to indirect light only.
  - A soft contact-shadow decal sits under each movable on lightmapped floors: footprint + 0.15 m, peak opacity
    0.35, linear falloff. Movables are not in the lightmaps.
  - The references show soft contact darkening under furniture and at wall bases (t069, ss_04), with no hard dark
    outlines.
- **Fog** (decided). None in clear weather: no distance haze is visible across the roofs in t059 or ss_06.
  - `FogExp2` colour is **scene-linear radiance**: sky colour (linear, max channel 1) × E_h × 0.5 / π, the radiance
    of a 50 % grey card under that sky. Set it with `fog.color.setRGB(r, g, b, THREE.LinearSRGBColorSpace)`.
    Values are in `out/rigs.json` `rigs.fog`.

    | rig | sky | E_h | colour (linear RGB) | density | fog at look point / far floor edge (60°) |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | storm night | 7000 K | 0.139 | (0.0201, 0.0197, 0.0221) | 0.012 per m | 1.6 % / 19.3 % |
    | storm day | 7000 K | 0.441 | (0.0636, 0.0625, 0.0702) | 0.012 per m | 1.6 % / 19.3 % |
    | cold wave | 6500 K | 0.441 | (0.0702, 0.0662, 0.0697) | 0.008 per m | 0.7 % / 9.1 % |

  - Density reasons (decided): storm 0.012 per m keeps the look point clear and greys the far floor edge by a
    fifth, so rain haze reads on the street, not in the home. Cold wave 0.008 is two-thirds of that because snow
    haze is thinner than rain.
  - Storm day and cold wave use E_h 0.441, the measured cold-wave level (decided as the overcast sky level;
    `rigs.overcast`).
  - Blackout and clear-weather interiors: no fog.

## 10. Reproduce

```sh
node tools/targets/fetch-targets.mjs [--appdetails research/store_appdetails.json] [--force]  # references + manifest (ffmpeg)
node tools/targets/fetch-targets.mjs --verify                     # re-hash every file
node tools/targets/check-scene-cache.mjs                          # scene-score cache: truncated / changed / forced
python3 -m venv tools/targets/.venv && tools/targets/.venv/bin/pip install -r tools/targets/requirements.txt
P=tools/targets/.venv/bin/python
$P tools/targets/camera.py && $P tools/targets/scale.py && $P tools/targets/measure.py && $P tools/targets/lut.py
$P tools/targets/boards.py contact
THREE_DIR=node_modules/three node tools/targets/validate-lut.mjs
```

The scene-score cache (`tools/targets/.cache/*.inner.scenes.txt`) is keyed to the remux's size and mtime and the
crop. It is reused only when complete (one score per frame, ending within two frames of the stream end), written
atomically, and cleared by `--force`.

## 11. Known gaps

- **Scale.** Absolute scale rests on the assumed 1.75 m / 1.65 m characters.
- **Wall height.** Three picks give 3.23–3.36 m, but only on the apartment and storeroom homes. The duplex has no
  native corner.
- **Assumed albedos.** The anchors plaster 0.60, white tile 0.75 and concrete 0.35 are assumed. Three rigs use
  frame-statistic anchors: dawn, night unlit, storm night.
- **Blackout.** t013 carries a screen-space darkness overlay; the blackout level is the lamp-free NIGHT set, not a
  measurement.
- **t035 time.** It shows the planning countdown, not the clock; it anchors only relative albedo.
- **Dusk (provisional).** Sun colour and direction rest on the t049 punch-in.
- **Night exterior (provisional).** Colour: the only street-at-night frame (t036) is a punch-in.
- **Sun angles.** Qualitative only: backlit day walls, the dusk slat direction. The transition path is specified
  (§4.3).
- **Lamp intensity.** The final bulb I comes from P0-08's LAMPS calibration (§4.5). 0.284 is only the t069
  starting value.
- **Lamps.** The lamp-pool method gives two t069 patches and a flat t033 wall. Intensity is derived and falloff is
  not claimed. The wall lamp and flashlight geometry are decided.
- **Hidden-roof shadows.** The bake contract (§4.4) assumes hidden geometry occludes, because no interior sun
  patches appear at 11:50 or 17:20.
- **Vignette (provisional).** Strength is unresolved: the HUD-only CI includes none.
- **UI.** Panel opacity is unmeasured, and small coloured text under-reads saturation (4:2:0).
- **Grain.** Measured through JPEG and H.264; below 0.3 codes it is not recoverable.
